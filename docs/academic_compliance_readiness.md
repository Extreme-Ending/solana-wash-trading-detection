## Academic-integrity controls already present

- missing outcomes and extraction metadata are stated instead of fabricated;
- heuristic flags are not promoted to ground truth;
- the anomaly score is not called a fraud probability;
- point-in-time feature construction and data-lineage leakage controls are testable;
- source, snapshot, registry, manifest and artifact hashes support local reproducibility;
- algorithm disagreement and repeated-wallet limitations are disclosed;
- synthetic fixtures are separated from research evidence;
- lecturer patterns are cited as adaptations and no lecturer data/result is claimed as original work; and
- Git operations remain student-owned.

## Required student actions before submission

1. Supply the primary proposal and rubric, then reconcile the research question, title, DEX domain and unsupervised method with those records.
2. Preserve written methodology authorization in the form required by the programme; do not rely only on an informal statement.
3. Resolve dataset source, licence and permitted academic-use evidence. If this cannot be done, agree on a lawful replacement or scope revision before publishing results.
4. Decide, under institutional policy, how to restore or otherwise provide the required factual AI-use disclosure. Phase 8E intentionally makes no such file.
5. Student-review all generated documentation, reproduce key commands, verify citations, and write the final interpretation in the student's own academically accountable voice.
6. Produce the final report bibliography, rubric evidence map and presentation without overstating anomaly results.
7. Decide whether ignored raw data and binary artifacts may be submitted, separately supplied, or must be regenerated by an examiner; document the lawful reproducibility route.
